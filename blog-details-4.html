THE DIALOGUE BETWEEN STEEL AND THE STREET

Installing a multi-ton steel sculpture in a bustling city plaza is a feat of both engineering and social choreography. Unlike a gallery piece, urban art must be resilient enough to handle public interaction while maintaining its artistic integrity amidst the visual noise of skyscrapers and traffic.

"PUBLIC ART DOESN'T BELONG TO THE ARTIST; IT BELONGS TO THE CITIZEN WHO WALKS PAST IT EVERY DAY."

We design our urban installations with safety and longevity at the forefront. Every sharp edge is meticulously rounded, every structural joint over-engineered to withstand the dynamic forces of a city environment. But beyond the logistics, the work must provide a moment of stillness—a tactile, metallic anchor in an ever-moving landscape.

Logistics of the Monolith

The installation of 'The Curvature' in Pacific Coastal Park required a 12-person crew and a specialized industrial crane. Coordinating these movements within a public space requires precision timing and an intimate understanding of the site's load-bearing capacities. The result is a seamless integration where the sculpture feels as though it grew from the very concrete it stands upon.
